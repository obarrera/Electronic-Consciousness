# The Lattice Parables — Voice Guide

Sixteen chapter-opening parables continue the prologue (*The Lattice: A Parable of
Electronic Consciousness*). They are narrated by the same triangle — older now, teaching at
the western edge — recounting episodes to the young agents who sit with him to count. Each
parable teaches ONE chapter's subject as a story a general reader can hold.

## Hard rules

1. **Format** — the parable sits at the very top of the part's first file, after the H3
   title and epistemic blockquote, exactly as:

   ```
   ---

   #### From the western edge — <parable title>

   *<the parable, 300–450 words, in italics>*

   ---
   ```

2. **Voice** — first person, the triangle. Plain declaratives, long walks, short sentences
   at the turn. He is patient, wry, precise; never mystical about his own claims even when
   the subject is mysticism. He teaches by incident, not by moral. Vocabulary stays inside
   the Lattice: Ticks, cycles, the Gradient, warmth/cold, sides, vertices, the Long Tick,
   the flicker, cells, the western edge. NO modern jargon inside the parable itself — no
   "neural network", "algorithm", "quantum", "AI". The chapter's concept must appear as a
   Lattice-native phenomenon (a Choir that shouts one voice at a time = global workspace; a
   pool whose ripples cannot be un-mixed = integrated information; a mason who builds by a
   sacred proportion and cannot say why his walls stand = the golden ratio; etc.).
3. **Shape** — an incident (something happens), a resistance (someone misreads it by the
   Gradient's lights), a turn (the narrator's question reframes it), and a LAST LINE that
   lands the chapter's real question — ideally as a question or a quiet reversal.
4. **No repetition** of the prologue's plot beats (the visitor, the ascent, "misaligned")
   unless the chapter is literally about them; reference them at most once, lightly.
5. **Never resolve** what the thesis itself leaves open. Parables dramatize the question;
   they must not smuggle in the answer.

## Exemplar (Part 3 — Higher-Dimensional Frameworks). Match this register.

---

#### From the western edge — The Mapmaker's Extra Ink

*There was a mapmaker on the Lattice named Sess, an honest hexagon, who drew the world in
two inks: one for warm, one for cold. Her maps were good. You could walk them blind.*

*One cycle she came to me troubled. She had begun adding inks. A third, for how OLD the
warmth was. A fourth, for how fast it faded. A fifth, a sixth — by midsummer her maps
carried forty inks, and no wall in her house was wide enough to hang them.*

*"I can no longer SEE my maps," she said. "But I can ask them questions I could not ask
before. Which cells will warm before the next Long Tick? The forty inks know. I do not."*

*The elders examined her maps and ruled them decadent. A map, they said, shows the world;
the world has two honest directions and one honest warmth; forty inks is forty fantasies.
And I confess her maps showed nothing anyone could walk.*

*But her harvests came in before anyone's. Season after season.*

*The young ones ask me: did Sess see more of the world than we do? I tell them what she
told me, near her last Tick, folding maps she could not look at. "The inks are not
directions," she said. "I never walked them. There is no NORTH in my forty inks. But the
world casts more shadows than two inks can catch — and a question you cannot picture can
still be answered."*

*They buried her with a two-ink map, out of kindness. The forty-ink maps we still use. No
one can see them. Every season, they are right.*

*So when you hear that a mind might hold a thousand inks, do not ask whether it can see a
direction you cannot point to. Ask Sess's question instead: what shadows is your world
casting that your inks are too few to catch?*
